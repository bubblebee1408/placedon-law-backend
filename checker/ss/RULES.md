# SS defect rules — evidenced from ROC adjudication orders

Every rule here is backed by at least one real penalty order. Rules with **zero** penalty precedent
are listed at the bottom as advisory-only and must never fire as a penalty-backed finding.

Corpus: 68 distinct s.454 adjudication orders, 2021-2026, harvested 19 Aug 2026.
Frequency = distinct orders in that corpus.

## Statutory basis

s.118(10) Companies Act 2013 makes SS-1 and SS-2 mandatory. s.118(11): **Rs 25,000 on the company and
Rs 5,000 on every officer in default.** No per-day continuing element under 118(11) — the
e-adjudication "Additional Penalty per day" column reads 0 in every s.118 order in the corpus.
s.446B halves it for small companies, startups, OPCs and producer companies.

## Penalty multiplies three ways — compute exposure per meeting and per book, never per company

1. **Per meeting.** Trouw Nutrition India: Rs 25,000 x 54 board meetings + Rs 25,000 x 7 general
   meetings + officers = **~Rs 21.35 lakh**, the largest in the corpus. Ground: minutes not entered
   within 30 days.
2. **Per financial year.** Anheuser Busch InBev Rs 1,50,000 over 3 FYs. Chongqing Jieli: two separate
   orders, one per FY, same defect.
3. **Per defect, per book, same day.** Om Shyamji Foods drew **three separate orders on 18.12.2025** —
   one each for the Board, AGM and EGM minutes books, same defect. Rosmerta Autotech drew **five
   orders on 09.10.2025**.

## Version-dating is mandatory, and there is authority for it

Regional Director, Hyderabad (Stanley Lifestyles, hearing 24.07.2025) held that **SS-1 was amended in
2017 to delete the requirement to disclose the number and dates of Board/Committee meetings and
director attendance in the Director's Report** — so a penalty on that ground was unwarranted.

Every check must therefore carry the SS version it implements and the date range it is valid for.
A check that fires against a repealed requirement is the same failure mode this product exists to
prevent.

## Enforcement volume — size the claim honestly

~20-25 s.118 / Secretarial Standards orders reach publication per year, roughly **4% of all published
adjudication orders**. Filing defaults (s.92/137) dominate at 16.2%. Enforcement is not slowing: 2026
is on pace to match or exceed 2025 at 8 months in.

Do not claim "high enforcement risk". Claim what the corpus supports: **the defence has never worked,
the penalty multiplies per meeting and per book, and it is entirely preventable.**
