Placedon · Companies Act 2013 · request for review

Twenty minutes of your judgement, on a tool that refuses to guess

We have built a compliance checker for Indian corporate law. It has never been seen by a practising Company Secretary or corporate lawyer. Before we build anything else, we would like you to tell us where it is wrong.

01

Start with a finding you can check yourself

ICSI's own Guidance Note on General Meetings publishes a specimen AGM notice. It still reads, in the resolution and again in the explanatory statement:

“…to hold office from the conclusion of this Annual General Meeting till the conclusion of the ____ Annual General Meeting of the company (subject to ratification of their appointment at every AGM), at a remuneration of Rs. _______/- … plus reimbursement of out of pocket expenses and service tax, as applicable.” Guidance Note on General Meeting (revised SS-2), Annexure II p.131; repeated at Annexure XVI p.163
Auditor ratificationThe first proviso to s.139(1) required ratification of the auditor's appointment at every AGM. It was omitted by the Companies (Amendment) Act 2017, s.40, with effect from 7 May 2018. A resolution drafted in these terms today states a requirement that no longer exists.
“service tax”Subsumed into GST from 1 July 2017.

We are not making a point about ICSI. We are making a point about templates. Every precedent in every firm drifts from the law the day it is saved, and the drift is invisible because the document still looks correct. That is the problem we are trying to solve, and the specimen above is the cleanest proof we have that it is real.

02

What it produces

One private company, financial year 2024-25, two directors, an AGM on 30 December 2025 following one on 20 August 2024, and three board meetings during 2025. The tool returns four rows, and each behaves differently on purpose.

s.96(1) — annual general meeting NOT SATISFIED
Hold an AGM, and within the statutory gap
Gap of 497 days from 2024-08-20 to 2025-12-30 exceeds the fifteen-month limit of 2025-11-20. This limb only — the first-AGM deadline and the Registrar's extension are separate limbs and are not decided here.
s.173(1) — board meetings UNDETERMINED
Hold the minimum number, correctly spaced
Three meetings would fail the standard regime. But the relaxed s.173(5) regime turns on small-company status, which we cannot establish — so we decline rather than judge this company on the stricter standard it may not owe.
s.149(1) — board size SATISFIED
Enough directors, and not more than the maximum
Two directors: at or above the private-company minimum of two and not above the maximum of fifteen. Numbers limbs only — the woman-director requirement is prescribed by rules we do not hold, so it is never decided here.
s.2(85) — small company CANNOT DETERMINE
Establish whether the company is a small company
The Act sets ₹50 lakh and ₹2 crore, then delegates the operative figures to rules. The commonly-quoted ₹4 crore / ₹40 crore come from G.S.R. 700(E) of 15 September 2022, which we have located but not properly acquired — so we refuse the arithmetic and name the instrument.

The last row is the one we most want you to react to. A chatbot would answer that question confidently from memory. We would rather say what is missing.

04

What we are not claiming

We would rather you knew the limits before you spend time on it.

  • No accuracy claim. The system has never been tested against real defective documents. Its internal benchmark is 69 constructed examples across five sections, reviewed by one person who is not a lawyer.
  • Four obligations, not the Act. Sections 96, 149, 173 and the s.2(85) classification. Everything else is unbuilt.
  • The rules are largely missing. We hold 529 sections of the Act and one set of subordinate rules. Many operative thresholds live in rules we have not acquired, which is why one row above refuses.
  • No language model is involved in any answer above. Every line is a provision, a fact supplied, or arithmetic on the two.
  • It is not a filing tool and does not connect to MCA.

If any of this is wrong, that is the most valuable thing you can tell us — a corrected refusal is worth more to us than a confirmed answer. There is no product to buy and nothing to sign up for.
