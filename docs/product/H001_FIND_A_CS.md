# Finding one Company Secretary to review — the part before the send

`H001_OUTREACH.md` assumes you already have someone to ask. This is the step
before that: how to find ten candidates and what to say when there is no warm
introduction. The goal is unchanged — one CS, twenty minutes, a reaction to the
evidence pack. This just removes "I don't know who to ask" as a reason it hasn't
happened.

You need **one who says yes**. Message ten; three or four will reply; one will
be interested. That is a normal cold-outreach funnel, not a failure.

---

## 1. Where to look, easiest first

**Your own network, one degree out.** Post once, publicly, on your own feed:

> Building a Companies Act compliance tool. Before I build further I want 20
> minutes with a practising Company Secretary to tell me where it's wrong — not
> a sales call, the opposite. If that's you or someone you know, please reply or
> DM.

A friend-of-a-friend intro converts far better than any cold message below. Try
this first and give it 48 hours.

The exact post, copy-paste ready (edit the bracket, nothing else):

> I've been building a compliance tool for the Companies Act, 2013, and before I
> take it further I want it torn apart by someone who does this work for real.
>
> If you're a practising Company Secretary — or you know one — I'd value 20
> minutes. Not a pitch, the opposite: I want to know where it's wrong. It even
> leads with a drafting defect in ICSI's own specimen AGM notice that you can
> check in under a minute.
>
> Reply or DM. [your name]

Post it once, publicly, from your own account. Tag no one; let it travel. Give it
48 hours before working the searches below.

**LinkedIn search.** Paste each into LinkedIn search, filter to your city and to
people, prefer small/independent practice over large firms (they feel the pain
the product is aimed at):

    "Company Secretary" "in practice" <your city>
    "Practising Company Secretary" <your city>
    "PCS" Companies Act <your city>
    ACS OR FCS "compliance" <your city>

The ACS / FCS suffix is the ICSI membership grade — a strong signal the person
actually holds the qualification rather than using the title loosely.

**ICSI chapter.** Your nearest ICSI chapter (icsi.edu → chapters) runs study
circles and events. A member doing small-company secretarial work is exactly the
profile, and "I'm a founder wanting a practitioner's view" is a normal reason to
attend or email the chapter.

**Practising-CS directories.** ICSI publishes a directory of members in
practice. A PCS in solo or small-firm practice is the ideal reviewer: they carry
the s.454 exposure personally and they do the drafting the tool is near.

---

## 2. The cold message

Shorter than the warm version, because a stranger owes you nothing and will
decide in one line whether to read on. Lead with what is in it for them (a look
at something in their field, and influence over it), not with what you want.

**LinkedIn DM / short email:**

> Hi [name] — I'm building a compliance-checking tool for the Companies Act,
> and I'm looking for one practising CS to tell me where it's wrong before I
> build any further. Not selling anything — genuinely the opposite, I want the
> holes found.
>
> It's a 20-minute look at a one-page demo, whenever suits you. It leads with a
> real drafting defect in ICSI's own specimen AGM notice that you can check in a
> minute. Worth a look?

**If they reply "what is it":**

> It takes a company's basic facts and produces a compliance position — one row
> per Companies Act obligation, each saying whether the duty applies, whether it
> looks met, or exactly what's missing. Then it renders that as a dated,
> cited record. I want you to tell me which rows a real practitioner would call
> wrong, and whether the "what we couldn't verify" list reads as useful or as
> the tool admitting it doesn't know much. One-page version: [link].

Do not oversell. "Tell me where it's wrong" is the honest ask and it is also the
one most likely to get a yes, because it flatters their expertise rather than
asking them to buy.

---

## 3. Why a CS says yes to this

Worth knowing, so the outreach does not read as a favour you are begging for:

- Practitioners like being asked for their judgement, especially by a builder
  who leads with "tell me where it's wrong" rather than "look what I made".
- The ICSI-specimen finding is a genuine hook — a defect in their own body's
  template is interesting to them regardless of the product.
- Twenty minutes is a small ask, and "before I build further" tells them their
  view actually changes something, which is true.

---

## 4. What counts as done

One structured session captured in `record_interview.py`, with per-row verdicts
and a reaction to the evidence pack's gap list. Not a testimonial — a list of
what the tool gets wrong and what a real matter needs that it does not have.

If the first CS says the whole framing is wrong for how the work is actually
done, that is the most valuable outcome available and it reorders everything
downstream. Do not defend the design in the room. The goal is to find out where
the system's model of legal work is wrong, while it is still cheap to change.
