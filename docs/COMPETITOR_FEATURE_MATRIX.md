# Competitor feature matrix — from vendor primary sources

Fetched 2026-09-01 from the vendors' own sites: `legora.com` and
`harvey.ai/products`. This replaces the earlier basis for competitor claims,
which was an unsourced document of unknown authorship — see
`COMPETITOR_PATTERN_ANALYSIS.md`, which classified several of its claims as
likely fabricated.

## The reading discipline this document holds to

A vendor's own site is a better source than an unsourced summary, and it is
still **marketing**. Two rules follow, and the second is the one most likely to
be broken in our favour:

1. A product name on a homepage tells us the surface exists and how the vendor
   wants it understood. It tells us nothing about how well it works.
2. **Absence from a marketing page is not absence from a product.** A feature
   we do not see listed may exist, be unlisted, be in beta, or be described in
   words we did not match. Every "not listed" cell below means exactly that and
   must never be read or quoted as "they cannot do this".

Neither site was crawled beyond its product page. Nothing here rests on
inference about internals: no architecture, no model choices, no retrieval
design. Those remain unverified and are out of scope for this document.

## What this actually says

**We are behind on everything a user touches and ahead on everything underneath
it.** Every `NONE` in that table is a product surface. Every `HELD` is
verification machinery. That is a real and uncomfortable summary: a lawyer
cannot use any of what we have built, because there is no way to put a document
in or get an answer out.

The four `HELD` rows in the lower block are not listed by either vendor. Under
rule 2 above, that is **not** evidence they lack them. What it does support is
narrower and still useful: these capabilities are not what either vendor leads
with, and a product whose headline is point-in-time correctness would be saying
something neither competitor's marketing says. Whether that is a gap in their
products or merely in their homepages is unknown and cannot be settled from
here.

The honest asymmetry is this. Their surfaces are ordinary software we could
build in weeks — grids, uploads, add-ins. Our verification core took months and
rests on corpus work, temporal reconstruction and source-defect handling that
does not transfer from a contract-review product. **The moat is real; the
product is missing.** Building surfaces is the tractable half.

## One competitor feature worth taking seriously

Legora's **Monitors** — "continuously scan global regulation and surfacing what
matters before it impacts your business" — is the nearest thing either vendor
lists to our statutory-currency thesis. It is forward-looking (alert me when
the law changes). Ours is backward-looking (what did the law say on the date
this meeting happened). They are complements, not the same feature, and the
backward-looking one is what a compliance audit needs. Do not assume they
cannot do both.

## What would settle the unverified items

- ROI and adoption figures: an independent study, not a vendor page.
- Whether either product does point-in-time law: a trial account, a demo, or
  documentation behind login. A homepage cannot answer it.
- Vault's real document ceiling: vendor documentation, not a third-party number.

Until then, no claim in this document may be used to say what a competitor
cannot do.
