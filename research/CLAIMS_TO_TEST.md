# Claims to test

Extracted from the simulated practitioner personas (L001–L100). **These are hypotheses, not
evidence** — the source material labels itself "in this simulation" and no interview has taken
place. They are listed here because a generated persona is a decent guess about what a real
practitioner might say, and a decent guess is a testable one.

Each claim is written so that a real practitioner can **refute it**. A question no one can answer
"no" to is not a question.

## Tier 1 — checkable against reality

These involve money, counts or a specific past event. A practitioner either pays that, files that
many, or remembers that happening. These are the only claims that can be strongly confirmed or
strongly killed in a 40-minute conversation.

| # | Claim (from persona) | Question that can be answered "no" | A "no" means |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Firm pays ~₹4L/yr for SCC Online *(L001)* | "What do you currently pay for legal research tools, and who signs off?" | No budget exists to redirect; pricing model is guesswork |
| T2 | Pays a consultant ₹20k/mo for notification alerts *(L004)* | "Do you pay anyone — person or service — to tell you when rules change?" | Alerting is not a purchased need |
| T3 | Hires a research assistant at ₹5,000/day for historical research *(L013)* | "Have you ever paid someone to work out what the law was on a past date?" | Historical reconstruction has no budget behind it |
| T4 | A missed amendment cost ₹50,000 in redone billable time *(L001)* | "Tell me about the last time you had to redo a document because a rule had changed." | The error either doesn't happen or doesn't cost |
| T5 | Files 40 forms per quarter *(L053)* | "How many filings did you personally do last quarter?" | Volume is too low for automation to matter |
| T6 | 96 sets of board minutes a year across 8 SPVs *(L015)* | "How many board meetings do you paper in a year?" | Same |
| T7 | Reviewer finds errors in ~30% of drafts *(L002)* | "When your work is checked, how often does the checker find something?" | Draft quality is not the pain |

## Tier 2 — behavioural, observed not asked

Do not ask these. **Watch for them** during the baseline task.

| # | Claim | What to observe | A "no" means |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Four-plus tabs to answer one question *(L001)* | Count the sources actually opened | Research is not fragmented |
| B2 | Historical version is an "archaeological dig" *(L009)* | Ask for FY 2018-19; watch what they do | Past-date questions are easy or rare |
| B3 | Won't delegate drafting because juniors miss amendments *(L026)* | "Who drafts this in your office?" | Verification isn't the delegation blocker |
| B4 | Won't trust AI output without a source link *(L004, L012)* | Do they open the cited section in the demo? | Either trust is higher than claimed — a **safety** finding — or lower |

## Tier 3 — stated preference, weakest

Personas are most confident and least reliable here. Treat any agreement as noise unless it comes
with a date, a document, or a name.

| # | Claim | Only counts if |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | "₹9,999/mo is the sweet spot" *(many)* | They name who approves it and what it replaces |
| P2 | "Verification badges shift liability" *(L001)* | They have actually been asked to evidence a date to a client |
| P3 | "I want one workspace, not five tools" *(L016)* | They can name the five, and what they'd stop paying for |

## The claim I most expect to be wrong

**T3 — the ₹5,000/day research assistant.** It is the most specific and the most convenient: it
prices the exact problem this product solves. Specific and convenient is the combination most likely
to be a persona's invention.

If three practitioners say "no, I'd just call a senior" — the historical-reconstruction thesis
weakens badly, and that is worth knowing in week one rather than month three.

## The claim that would most change the plan

**B2.** If experienced practitioners answer a past-date question in under two minutes from memory or
a single source, then amendment reconstruction is not a product. If they can't answer it at all, it
is the whole product.
